
# Prompt Engineering Guide — GenAI, LLMs & Prompt Engineering

> `Prompt engineering` is the practice of articulating prompts to get the best response from a generative AI model. The better structured a prompt is, the better the output will be. A `prompt` is the text you feed to the model — it can be a question, instruction, or cue that initiates a specific action or response. #prompt_engineering #GenAI #LLM


---


## 1. Core Definitions

### Generative AI

- **Definition:** a subset of artificial intelligence that is capable of creating `text, images, or other data` using generative models, often in response to prompts
- **Key distinction:** generative AI encompasses a `broader range of models` capable of generating various types of content beyond just text
- AI has been around since the `mid 1950s`, but gen AI grew in popularity hugely since `2021`
- gen AI models learn the `patterns and structure` from input training data and then `create new data` with similar characteristics
- **Industries:** software development, healthcare, finance, entertainment, customer service, sales

### Large Language Models (LLMs)

- **Definition:** large, general-purpose language models that can be `pre-trained and then fine-tuned` for specific purposes — a `subset of generative AI` focusing specifically on language tasks
- **"Large" refers to two things:**
	- the size of the `training dataset` (sometimes at the `petabyte scale`)
	- the number of `parameters` — the memories and knowledge the machine learned during training; can reach `billions or even trillions`
- **"General-purpose"** means the models can sufficiently solve common problems thanks to the `commonality of human language`
- **"Pre-trained and fine-tuned"** means:
	- `pre-trained` for a general purpose with a `large dataset`
	- `fine-tuned` for specific goals with a `much smaller dataset`

> `NOTE:` while "generative AI" and "LLM" are often used interchangeably, they're `not identical` — GenAI is the umbrella category; LLMs are a specific type of GenAI focused on language

## 2. How LLMs Work

- when you submit a prompt, the LLM `calculates the probability` of the correct answer from its pre-trained model
- **pre-training** involves feeding a massive dataset of `text, images, and code` to the model so it can learn the `underlying structure and patterns` of language
- in this way, the LLM works like a `fancy autocomplete` — suggesting the most common correct response to the prompt

### Hallucinations

- **Definition:** words or phrases generated by the model that are often `nonsensical or grammatically incorrect`, or factually wrong
- **Why hallucinations happen:** LLMs `can only understand the information they were trained on`:
	- they might not be aware of your `business's proprietary or domain-specific data`
	- they do not have access to `real-time information`
	- they assume the prompt is `true`
	- they do not have the ability to `ask for more context`
	- an LLM `does not know anything outside of what it was trained on`
- **Causes of hallucinations:**
	- model is not trained on `enough data`
	- model is trained on `noisy or dirty data`
	- model is not given `enough context`
	- model is not given `enough constraints`

## 4. Prompt Categories

### Zero-Shot Prompts

- **Definition:** contain `no context or examples` to assist the model
- _Example:_ `"What's the capital of France?"`
- works well for `simple, well-known questions`

### One-Shot Prompts

- **Definition:** provide `one example` to the model for context
- _Example:_ `"The capital of Italy is Rome. What's the capital of France?"`

### Few-Shot Prompts

- **Definition:** provide `at least two examples` to the model for context
- _Example:_ `"The capital of Italy is Rome. The capital of Japan is Tokyo. What's the capital of France?"`

### Role Prompts

- **Definition:** require a `frame of reference` (persona) for the model to work from as it answers
- _Example:_ `"I want you to act as a business professor. I'll give you a term, and you will correctly explain its meaning. Make sure your answers are always right. What is ROI?"`
- role prompts are often the `best solution for technical tasks` because they give the LLM a clear point of reference

## 5. Anatomy of a Prompt

A prompt has two main elements:

### Preamble

- the `introductory text` that gives the model context and instructions before the main request
- think of it as `setting the stage` for the LLM
- can include:
	- **context** for the task
	- **the task itself**
	- **examples** to guide the model

### Input

- the `central request` you're making to the LLM
- what the instruction or task will `act upon`
- _Example:_ `"Comment: I don't know what to think about the video. The review is:"` — based on the preamble, Gemini reviews the input and suggests if the review is positive, neutral, or negative

> `NOTE:` not all components are required for every prompt, and the `format and element order can change` depending on the task

## 6. Prompt Engineering Best Practices

- **write detailed and explicit instructions** — the more vague the prompt, the more chance the model produces `unusable results`; be clear and concise
- **define boundaries** — instruct the model on `what to do`, rather than what not to do; if the model gets stuck, give it `fallback outputs` (e.g., `"I'm still learning about that"`)
- **adopt a persona** — adding a persona provides `meaningful context` that helps the model focus on related questions and `improve accuracy`
- **keep each sentence concise** — longer sentences can produce `suboptimal results`; break long sentences into a `series of shorter sentences and simpler tasks`

### Sasha's Refined Prompt — Putting It All Together

The training follows Sasha, a cloud architect prototyping a VPC network architecture. Her prompt evolved:

**v1 (vague):**
> `"How can I create a network that uses IPv4 and IPv6 addresses?"`

**v2 (role + specificity):**
> `"I want you to act as a cloud architect in Google Cloud. How can I use gcloud to create a network that uses IPv4 and IPv6 subnets?"`

**v3 (full best practices — role + context + constraints):**
> `"You're a cloud architect. You want to build a Google Cloud VPC network that can be centrally managed. You also connect to other VPC networks in your company's other regions. You don't want to have many different sets of firewall policies to maintain. What sort of network architecture would you recommend?"`

**Result:** Gemini proposed a `hub-and-spoke network architecture`, which fit the requirements exactly.

> `NOTE:` this progression from v1 → v3 is a textbook example of iterative prompt refinement — each version adds `role`, `context`, and `constraints`, which dramatically improves the quality and relevance of the response.

## 7. Tips & Tricks

- **iterate, don't expect perfection on the first prompt** — refine your prompts based on the model's responses, adding context, constraints, and examples with each iteration
- **Gemini maintains interaction context** — you can build on previous responses rather than repeating the full prompt each time (e.g., `"How can I adjust the gcloud command provided to ensure the subnet is dual stack?"`)
- **use role prompts for all technical tasks** — especially when generating `gcloud commands`, `YAML configs`, or `architecture recommendations`
- **validate all LLM outputs** — especially for `infrastructure-as-code` and `gcloud commands`; hallucinated parameters or flags can cause real damage if applied without review
- **provide fallback instructions** — for agentic workflows where the LLM might get stuck, always include fallback behaviors so the system degrades gracefully
- **exam tip:** know the four prompt categories (`zero-shot`, `one-shot`, `few-shot`, `role`) and be able to identify which one a given prompt example falls into
